# tiny-chess-01 engine notes

tiny-chess-01 is a pocket-sized Sunfish descendant that treats a hard budget of 15000 nodes as its entire personality: no clocks, no thread pools, no aspiration drama — just a deterministic MTD-bi driver that squeezes a zero-window search until the score converges. Every run on the same position replays the identical node trace, because the move ordering, the table probes, and the bisection pivots are all pure functions of the board state and the node counter. The engine leans on Sunfish's compact piece-square evaluation and rotated-mailbox style board handling, but where classic Sunfish iterates deepening with a widening window, this one bisects: each pass guesses a bound, searches it with a null window, and lets the fail-high or fail-low result steer the next guess. The result is an engine small enough to read in one sitting yet stubborn enough to grind out an exact minimax value within its fixed 15000-node allowance, and reproducible down to the last quiescence probe.

## Search details

- Null-window bisection: MTD-bi keeps upper and lower score bounds and repeatedly searches a zero-width window around a pivot guess; a fail-high raises the lower bound, a fail-low lowers the upper bound, and the loop terminates when the bounds meet, yielding the exact score without ever needing a full-width re-search.
- Transposition-table bound caching: each probe stores the score together with a flag marking it as an exact value, a lower bound (fail-high), or an upper bound (fail-low), plus depth and best move; on a revisit the entry only produces a cutoff when its bound type is compatible with the current null window, and its stored move feeds ordering even when no cutoff fires — which is what makes successive bisection passes cheap.
- The cutoff trio prunes the tree ahead of the table: futility drops quiet moves at frontier nodes whose static eval plus a margin cannot reach the window, late-move reductions shave depth on late-ordered quiet moves and re-search at full depth only if the reduced search surprises, and null-move pruning hands the opponent a free tempo at sufficient depth to prove whole subtrees fail high — together they keep the 15000-node budget spent on lines that can actually move a bound.
